I’d think about client-financed acquisition as self-liquidating acquisition: the first client should generate enough gross profit and collected cash to fund the next one fast enough that growth compounds instead of draining cash.

In your case, the economics are strong. With about `$1k` CAC and about `$10k` of first-30-day gross profit, payback is roughly `1 month`. That means the main constraint is not unit economics; it’s cash timing and delivery capacity.

So the practical rule is:

- If the client’s upfront payment and early gross profit cover acquisition spend quickly, the acquisition is effectively financed by the client.
- If you have to front spend before cash collects, you still need working capital.
- But with these numbers, the model supports fairly aggressive acquisition as long as fulfillment can keep up.
