Because ads are paid for out of gross profit, and gross profit is what's left after fulfillment cost, not out of revenue. When you charge for a project, part of that money immediately goes to delivering it (procurement, install, Lisa's time); only what's left over can be used to run the business, including buying customers [gross-profit:0][gross-profit:4]. So fulfillment cost sets the size of the pool that has to cover your acquisition spend. Whether ads work is decided by three numbers, CAC, gross profit, and payback period, and fulfillment cost lives inside gross profit and inside the monthly payback math, since each month you subtract the cost to deliver before anything is left to recover CAC [how-businesses-make-money:2][payback-period:0][payback-period:2]. Without fulfillment cost we cannot compute gross profit, so we cannot tell whether a given CAC pays back or whether ads would lose money on every client.
