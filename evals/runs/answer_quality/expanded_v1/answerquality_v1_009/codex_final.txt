Yes, I would use this as a conditional “win your program fee back” offer, but I would strongly prefer earning it back as credit rather than a cash refund.

The clean version is: clients pay the $2,400 program fee upfront. If they hit a specific, objective 12-week result and complete the required check-ins, they earn up to $2,400 back as credit toward ongoing training or a renewal. That matches the Money Models pattern: set a goal, show the client how to reach it, and let them qualify for money back or store credit when they hit it [win-your-money-back:0].

The check-ins should be qualification requirements, not administrative chores. For example: baseline assessment, weekly progress check-ins, nutrition compliance review, midpoint adjustment, final measurement, photos/testimonial if they succeed. The source material supports tying refund eligibility to the actions required to get the result, like attendance, showing up, and turning in data [pay-less-now:5]. It also supports requiring check-ins because they are what make the result more likely [free-trials:6].

I would avoid a broad cash-back promise at first. Your first-30-day gross profit is already $900 against a $320 CAC, so acquisition economics are strong, but a full $2,400 cash refund creates real delivery and cash-flow exposure. Credit is better because it preserves the risk reversal, pushes the client into continuity, and keeps the money inside the business.

My recommended offer language would be: “Complete the 12-week transformation, attend every required check-in, submit your weekly nutrition/training data, and hit your agreed strength/body-composition target. When you do, we credit your full $2,400 program fee toward your next phase of coaching.”

I would pilot it with tight rules: one measurable result per client, objective before/after measurement, no partial subjective disputes, and credit issued only after all check-ins and final proof are complete. If you want a cash version later, cap it or test it with a small cohort first.
