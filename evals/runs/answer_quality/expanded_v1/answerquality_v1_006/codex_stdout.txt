We are diagnosing payback period and client-financed acquisition, not whether the software has long-term value.

Your lifetime economics look strong: $18,000 lifetime gross profit against $2,800 CAC. The issue is timing. Month-one gross profit is $1,100, so each new customer still leaves $1,700 unrecovered at the start, and the payback period is about 2.9 months.

In Money Models terms, payback period asks how long it takes gross profit to exceed CAC, and shorter payback makes acquisition easier to scale [payback-period:0]. Client-financed acquisition asks whether first-30-day gross profit can cover CAC, so growth can recycle customer cash instead of tying up yours [cfa:0].

So the diagnosis is: you have a cash-conversion gap in acquisition. The next work is to either lower CAC or pull more gross profit into the first 30 days through a setup/onboarding fee, prepayment, earlier upsell, or a stronger front-end offer.
