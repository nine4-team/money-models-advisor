If the $597 diagnostic sells, I would offer the $5,000 pre-designed room package next, positioned as the immediate implementation path for the biggest room or highest-ROI upgrade the diagnostic uncovers.

The Money Models reason is simple: once the attraction offer is working, the next lever for first-month profit is an upsell, because upsells are the offer that comes after the first sale and are specifically used to maximize 30-day profit [upsell-offers:1]. The build sequence is attraction offer first, then upsell, then downsell, then continuity [make-your-money-model:2].

I would not jump straight to the full $15,000 design offer as the only next step. Offer it as the premium option, but make the main next offer: “Apply your $597 diagnostic credit toward a $5,000 room plan if you choose your room within 7 days.” That uses a rollover upsell: credit some or all of the customer’s prior purchase toward the next offer [rollover-upsell:1]. The framework also says the next offer should generally be at least 4x the credit so there is still profit left after the discount [rollover-upsell:6]. Here, $5,000 is about 8.4x the $597 diagnostic, so the credit is economically reasonable before fulfillment costs.

My exact next offer would be: “Your diagnostic found the fastest profit opportunity. We can turn that into a done-for-you room package. If you start this week, your $597 diagnostic applies toward the package. Most owners use this for the primary bedroom, living room, or the weakest photo-performing room.”

That should increase first-month profit more cleanly than adding a new low-ticket product, because it monetizes the trust created by the diagnostic while the problem is fresh.
