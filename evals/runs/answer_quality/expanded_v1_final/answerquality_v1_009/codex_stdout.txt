The clean version is a “win your money back” attraction offer: they pay for the 12-week program, qualify to earn the fee back only if they hit a specific measurable result, complete the required check-ins, and give you usable proof/testimonial assets. That matches the framework: the customer pays upfront, you set the goal and path, and they can qualify for money back or credit when they reach it [win-your-money-back:0].

For this business, I would prefer credit back over full cash back at first. A $2,400 cash refund to successful clients can become expensive fast because you still carry fulfillment cost. A $2,400 credit toward ongoing training, an annual membership, or a higher-support package preserves the result-based promise while keeping cash and retention healthier. Your current economics are strong enough to test bold risk reversal, but not enough to ignore redemption math.

The check-ins should be part of the eligibility rules, not an arbitrary hoop. The source material supports requiring the activities that actually create the result: nutrition/progress/transformation meetings, homework, stats, group posts, or final proof assets [free-trials:6] [free-trials:2].
